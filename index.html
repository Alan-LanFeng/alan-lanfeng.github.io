About

I am a PhD candidate at EPFL, Switzerland, working with Prof. Alexandre Alahi at the VITA Lab. Before EPFL I received my M.Sc. in Robotics, Systems and Control from ETH Zurich and B.E. in Navigation Engineering from Wuhan University.

I have built synthetic training data for physical AI since before my PhD. For my master's thesis at the AIT Lab, ETH Zurich, with Prof. Otmar Hilliges and NVIDIA, I trained human-to-robot handover policies on synthetic hand-object motions (SynH2R). Earlier, with Prof. Bolei Zhou at UCLA, I worked on generative simulation for reinforcement learning.

My current research makes generation itself cheap. RDM trains one-step generators by matching feature distributions under frozen encoders, and turns the four-step FLUX.2 [klein] into a one-step model that beats its teacher. PTD, from my internship at NVIDIA Research, distills the 33B audio-video model MiniMax-H3 into a four-step generator with LoRA alone; its videos are more diverse and more natural than those from DMD-style distillation, at matching motion quality. On the data side, TAROT (ICML 2025) selects training data with optimal transport, and RAP (ICLR 2026) shows that policies can be trained on cheap 3D rasterizations instead of photorealistic renders. I have also worked on minute-scale human animation (EverAnimate, NeurIPS 2026) and on world models that let a robot plan toward goals given in language (Grounded World Model).

Next, I am combining these into world models that are fast enough for closed-loop training and that generate the situations a policy fails on. I am looking for a research internship in 2027, and I am open to collaborations on these topics.

Generative models

Parametric Trajectory Distillation for Few-Step Video Generation
Lan Feng, Peter Karkus, Maximilian Igl, Julius Berner, Yuxiao Chen, Shuhan Tan, Alexandre Alahi, Boris Ivanovic, Marco Pavone
arXiv 2026  ·  w/ NVIDIA Research
Four-step PTD on MiniMax-H331 s instead of 343 s per clip
Few-step distillation for large video diffusion and flow models: the student fits each denoising segment with a polynomial curve under a single least-squares loss and keeps the teacher's architecture. On the 33B audio-video MiniMax-H3, PTD trained with LoRA alone samples in four steps instead of 50 (31 s instead of 343 s for a five-second clip with sound on H100 GPUs). Against the DMD-based LightX2V Turbo it raises judged seed diversity from 1.55 to 2.81 at matching motion quality, and blinded raters give it a 63.4% preference share.

Representation Distribution Matching for One-Step Visual Generation
Lan Feng, Wuyang Li, Éloi Zablocki, Matthieu Cord, Alexandre Alahi
arXiv 2026
Teacher, 4 stepsRDM, 1 step
A one-step image generator trained by matching feature distributions under frozen encoders, with no online teacher, adversary, trajectory, or JVP. Sets the one-step state of the art on ImageNet under SWr14, a Sliced-Wasserstein distance over 14 frozen encoders, and post-trains the four-step FLUX.2 [klein] into a one-step model that beats it on GenEval (0.826 vs 0.794) and PickScore (22.76 vs 22.58) in 90 H200 GPU-hours.

EverAnimate: Minute-Scale Human Animation via Latent Flow Restoration
Wuyang Li, Yang Gao, Mariam Hassan, Lan Feng, Wentao Pan, Po-Chien Luan, Alexandre Alahi
NeurIPS 2026
Wan-Animate at 90 sEverAnimate at 90 s
Minute-scale human animation with less drift: a persistent latent memory carries identity and motion across chunks, and restorative flow matching refines each chunk during sampling, with LoRA tuning only. At 90 seconds it reduces LPIPS by 32% and FID by 27% against prior long-animation methods.

World models and data for physical AI

Grounded World Model: Latent Planning with Language Goals
Quanyi Li, Lan Feng, Haonan Zhang, Wuyang Li, Letian Wang, Alexandre Alahi, Harold Soh
arXiv 2026
“Pick up the tool used to clean shoes”
A latent world model that imagines the outcome of candidate actions and scores it against a language instruction in a shared vision-language space, so a robot can plan toward goals given in words. In simulation (WISER) it solves 87% of 288 tasks with unseen instructions, against 22% on average for ten fine-tuned VLA models, and on a real Franka arm it completes 55 of 60 pick-and-place sub-tasks.

RAP: 3D Rasterization Augmented End-to-End Planning

TAROT: Targeted Data Selection via Optimal Transport
Lan Feng*, Fan Nie*, Yuejiang Liu, Alexandre Alahi
ICML 2025 🏆 nuScenes Prediction #1
Target, CityscapesNearest GTA5 frame
Selects the training subset whose optimal-transport distance to a target domain is smallest, measured with whitened feature distances. Beats prior selection methods on semantic segmentation, motion prediction, and LLM instruction tuning, and models trained on its subsets match or beat those trained on the full dataset.

UniTraj: A Unified Framework for Scalable Vehicle Trajectory Prediction
Lan Feng*, Mohammadhossein Bahari*, Kaouther Messaoud Ben Amor, Éloi Zablocki, Matthieu Cord, Alexandre Alahi
ECCV 2024 🏆 nuScenes Prediction #1
Unifies datasets, models, and metrics across Waymo, Argoverse, and nuScenes for vehicle trajectory prediction. Shows that models degrade sharply on unseen datasets, and that training on more, and more diverse, data gives a new state of the art on nuScenes.

SynH2R: Synthesizing Hand-Object Motions for Learning Human-to-Robot Handovers
Sammy Christen*, Lan Feng*, Wei Yang, Yu-Wei Chao, Otmar Hilliges, Jie Song
ICRA 2024  ·  w/ ETH AIT Lab & NVIDIA
Synthetic human motionReal robot
Trains human-to-robot handover policies on synthetic hand-object motions instead of motion-capture data. A handover-aware grasp synthesis method generates training and test data with 100x more objects than prior work, and the policy trained purely on synthetic data is competitive with methods that use real human motion, in simulation and on a real robot.

Quanyi Li*, Zhenghao Peng*, Lan Feng*, Zhizheng Liu, Chenda Duan, Wenjie Mo, Bolei Zhou

Supports large-scale scenario generation, imitation learning and reinforcement learning.
